Environment and Climate Change is the federal department responsible for environmental protection, conservation of wildlife and natural habitats, monitoring air and water quality, and addressing climate change. It also oversees related portfolio agencies, including Parks Canada, which manages the national parks and historic sites system, and the Canadian Environmental Assessment Agency, which reviews the environmental effects of major projects before they proceed.

In the 2014-15 fiscal year, total spending attributed to the department was {{totalSpending}}, representing {{percentageOfFederal}} of total federal spending for the year. This spending was authorized across nine separate appropriation votes, covering program expenditures, operating expenditures, capital expenditures, grants and contributions, and payments to the New Parks and Historic Sites Account.

Spending was divided primarily between the Department itself and the Parks Canada Agency, with a smaller amount attributed to the Canadian Environmental Assessment Agency. This structure reflects the department's role in setting environmental policy and regulation through the Department, while delivering park operations and visitor services through a separate operating agency.

Its largest transfer payment categories supported contributions in support of biodiversity, wildlife and habitat, and contributions in support of sustainable ecosystems, along with the Habitat Stewardship Contribution Program, the National Conservation Plan's Natural Areas Conservation Program Grant, and contributions in support of climate change and clean air. These transfer payments were made up almost entirely of contributions, with one grant program among the largest categories.
